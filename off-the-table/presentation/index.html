MODALITY: ARMS & HANDS

Off The Table

You are a cat. Knock the humans' nice things off the table with your hand, and don't get caught.

Marie  marie-6.github.io/fun-games/off-the-table

Hi, I'm Marie. My game is called Off The Table. You play a cat whose only goal in life is to knock the humans' nice things onto the floor. You control the cat's paw with your own hand through the webcam. Feel free to open the link and play along.

THE MODALITY

I picked arms & hands

Your hand becomes the cat's paw. Webcam hand tracking reads your hands, and the camera image is never shown. Right- or left-handed, you choose.

Hand open

Hold it at chest height (the table) to put the paw out.

Sweep sideways

The paw follows your hand and swipes things off.

Drop it

Hand out of view: act innocent. Nothing to see here.

Fist or open mouth

Meow with your other fist, or open wide. Cute if they're watching.

I chose arms and hands. Using webcam hand tracking, the game watches both of your hands. You pick right- or left-handed first. An open hand at chest height puts the paw out, sweeping sideways swipes, and dropping the hand out of view is acting innocent. Your other hand makes a fist to meow, or you can just open your mouth wide. I deliberately never show the camera image: only the cat's paw moves on screen.

WHY ARMS

A cat's swipe is already an arm gesture.

It maps 1:1

Reach out, swipe, pull back. Players don't need to learn a control, they just do what a cat does.

Guilt is physical

Freezing when the door opens is funnier when your whole arm has to drop and go still.

Works at a desk

Hand tracking works sitting at a laptop. Your arm does the sweep, your hand tells the game what you mean.

[Edit into your own words.] Why arms interested me: the cat's swipe is literally an arm motion, so the control explains itself. The comedy of the game is the guilty freeze, and that freeze becomes real when your body has to stop. And hand tracking works when you're sitting at a laptop: the arm makes the big sweep, and the hand (open or fist) says what you mean.

USER INTERFACE

How arms shaped the interface

A paw as long as your arm

The paw stretches from the cat to your hand, mirrored, so you see your arm in the cat.

Readable from a step back

Thick outlines, big objects, few colors.

Warnings you can hear

Your eyes are on the paw, so footsteps warn you before the door opens.

Rules, not a video

No camera feed on screen. One card explains every gesture first.

For the interface: the paw is drawn as a long stretchy arm that follows your hand, mirrored like a mirror, so your movement and the cat's feel like one. The art is a chunky poster style so it's readable even if you lean back from the laptop. Because your eyes are on your paw, the footsteps sound is the warning before the door opens. And since I don't show the camera, one card explains every gesture before you start.

[Edit into your own words.] After the first version, I reworked the camera controls. Now the game explains every gesture on one screen before you start, and asks whether you're right- or left-handed: one hand swipes, the other only meows, so meowing never moves the paw by accident. You can meow by raising your other fist, or just by opening your mouth wide. I also fixed tracking bugs: the paw popping out while you were acting innocent, and flicker. The camera image is still never shown.

WITH MORE TIME

What I'd work on next

Calibration

A quick "show me your reach" step so tall, short, sitting and standing players all reach the whole table.

Smoother tracking

Filter the jitter so a shaky but frozen arm doesn't read as moving.

More cat moves

A two-handed push, a slow nudge to the edge, a tail flick, each its own arm gesture.

Playtesting with arms

Watch more people play to tune difficulty and how long a round should last.

[Edit to match your own plans.] With more time I'd add a calibration step for different body sizes and seating positions, smooth out tracking jitter, design more arm gestures for different cat moves, and run more playtests specifically with arm control.
